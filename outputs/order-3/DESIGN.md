# ORDER//3 — ACT 30 design contract

The playable prototype has three allies, three enemies, a shared twelve-card deck and five-card hands. A player queues one to three commands, with every card also usable for a one-tile allied move. Ownership limits printed techniques; a dead owner's card becomes a common unlimited-range +2 armor order for one living ally. A card queued as a printed technique does not convert if its owner dies during execution.

Enemy targeting is fixed when the turn begins: nearest living ally by orthogonal distance, then lower HP, then Rook, Vale, Iona. The announced unit and rune coordinates never retarget. There are no speed bands. Actions resolve in six fixed slots: ally 1, enemy 1, ally 2, enemy 2, ally 3, enemy 3 (rank: ally = queue index x 2, enemy = (slot - 1) x 2 + 1). Ally numbers are queue order. Empty slots create no event and are skipped. Returning a middle queued order moves the later orders up one number. A unit defeated before its slot misfires and never switches card or target. The preview and execution apply the same resolver snapshots. A selected card inserts a provisional action into the next ally slot, so legal targets are computed from the state after ally 1..n and enemy 1..n. Results, invalidations and movement traces are selectable in the timeline.

The enemy team shares a 15-card deck, 5 cards per enemy (the Copies column below), shuffled by its own mulberry32 stream (enemy seed = battle seed XOR 0x9E3779B9 unless a test override is set), so the ally stream is unchanged. The enemy draws five cards at battle start and at each turn start after the ally draw, all five are public, and at turn end all five (chosen, unused and defeated enemies' cards) go to the enemy discard; an empty deck reshuffles the discard. Fifteen cards drawn five at a time appear exactly once in turns 1-3, and the deck reshuffles on turn 4 (and every three turns after). Defeated enemies' cards stay in the deck, hand and discard rotation and cannot be chosen. Once per turn, before planning, commitEnemyPlan evaluates each hand card on the turn-start state only (it never reads the player's hand, plan or forecast, and nothing re-evaluates during planning or resolution): a lethal reachable attack +100; a reachable attack damage x 10 (Drain adds healable HP x 5, up to 2 x 5); Detonate 40 per living ally on a rune cell (3 with none; +100 if one of them has 4 HP or less); a guard whose protected unit has a living ally within 5 cells armor x 5, otherwise 2; Inscribe 15 per living ally on its cross (unusable while runes remain); an out-of-reach approach 5 + steps; an attack with no path and no adjacency is unusable, Detonate without runes is unusable. Cards are ranked by score, card number, hand index, and taken in that order under four limits: one card per name, two cards per enemy, at most as many orders as living enemies (3/2/1), and defeated enemies' cards never. The chosen cards are ordered guard, attack, rune, then by score, then by adoption, and are numbered back-filled: three cards take enemy 1-3, two take enemy 2-3, one takes enemy 3. Enemy events are keyed by card instance, so one enemy's two cards resolve in their own slots, the second from where the first moved; if the enemy falls before its first card both are cancelled and no unused card moves up. Hostile runes stay until a detonation resolves, and also clear when the Cantor's detonation is cancelled because it is down, and at the end of any turn in which the Cantor is down.

There are only three temporary unit effects. Armor stacks and absorbs before HP. Pin prevents movement but allows an adjacent attack. Mark adds 3 to the next ally-origin damage to its enemy, then is consumed even when armor absorbs all damage; it cannot stack. Arc Spark checks each damaged enemy's mark independently. Armor, pin and mark clear at turn end; HP and positions persist. An unused Ember Trap also expires then. Hostile runes remain until a detonation card resolves (see the enemy deck rules above).

Card definitions use side-neutral target types (nearest opponent, preferred friend or self, self, cross cells on the nearest opponent, rune cells) so they can later be used by allies; the Cover preference for the Cantor and runes hurting only the placing side's opponents are enemy-only premises for now. Pommel Break removes only armor; it does not cancel a detonation. Enemy movement uses an empty path around walls and units. Ember Trap triggers once, deals 3, consumes itself and stops remaining steps of that movement event; an already adjacent attack can still occur. New trap placement on an occupied trap cell replaces it, never stacks.

The battle screen is a fixed card-game table: the five public enemy cards on top (chosen orders 1-3 first with a number badge, fixed target and short effect, then unused cards with their reason or 「撃破済み」; a chosen card opens the same event view and trace as its forecast cell, an unused card opens an enemy-card view with the reason, the card's copies out of the 15 enemy cards (「この札は敵の札15枚中N枚」, not the current deck count) and evaluation, and the selected forecast cell and its card share a dashed .linked mark), the enemy deck and discard counts top left, the six-slot forecast and the own deck/discard counts on the left, the field in the middle, the selected ally, card or event details with Execute and Undo on the right, and the hand right-aligned below them, with registered orders kept in the hand row as numbered placed cards. The layout is the same at every size (landscape play is assumed; no portrait layout), the page does not scroll on landscape screens, and only the height band changes sizes and how much text is visible. In the right column the operations come first (return a cancelled order, the selected ally's commands, card use/move/deselect, Interpose target registration) and explanations, effect details, states and the log follow; when the column body has more below, a fade with 「続き」 marks its bottom edge. Returning a cancelled order is offered in both the plan and the selected-ally view, and a registration that fills the plan (three orders) or leaves the selected ally with no usable command switches the column back to the plan view. A tap or click on a unit token, status chips included, is always the board operation (select the ally or the target); status text shows on mouse hover, on keyboard focus of the cell, and in the right column (the selected ally's statuses, or the statuses of the units on highlighted target cells). Every visible letter is at least 10px, including unit tokens (small cells show the icon and current HP only); on narrow top rows enemy card names shorten and the full text stays in the right column and the accessible names. On screens 700px and taller the forecast's second line (target and forecast) wraps instead of being cut.

Cards show name, target/range and one numeric effect sentence. Help carries full conditions. The actor-first hand view, ALT, queue return, Interpose candidate preview, enemy movement trace, optional opening guide, status inspection and local notes are retained. The notes workflow distinguishes local save from explicit form submission; opening the form is not delivery. Each battle takes one uint32 seed; mulberry32 streams (the ally stream and the derived enemy stream) are the only shuffle sources. A local play log stores the seed, per-turn start and result checkpoints (including draw and discard pile order), the execution-time hand order and committed orders, so a run can be replayed through the same forecast and post-resolution path as live play. A replayed turn counts as verified only when both its start and result checkpoints match; a committed turn without a recorded result is incomplete, and a result without a commit is invalid. A run records at most 60 turns and is marked truncated only at that limit; only such a run, with its 60th turn committed and resolved, skips the recorded-status check, because the battle can end after its recorded turns. Play-log schema v2 drops the speed key and records the enemy seed, enemy rng calls, enemy hand, enemy deck and discard order, and slot-numbered enemy intents keyed by card instance. Schema v1 runs (ACT 26-29) stay readable, exportable and commentable, and replay only on their own product version. The storage key prefix order3.playlog.v1.run. names the storage namespace, not the schema version. The play log is never sent over the network. The product works as a static page and needs no gameplay service.
